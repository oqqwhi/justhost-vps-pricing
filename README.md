Two things worth noticing. The saving scales with the plan, so on the big plans the annual prepay is a four‑figure decision in absolute terms even though the percentage never moves. And the discount is identical across all 18 plans, which means it tells you nothing about which plan is good value — that comparison has to come from the specs.

On specs, Sirius Special costs $2.25 per GB of RAM. Arcturus Special costs $3.21 for 2 GB, or $1.61 per GB, and adds 10 GB of NVMe for $0.96 more per month. If your project is a small site with a database rather than a static page, Arcturus is the better buy despite the higher sticker. Vega Special lands at $1.56 per GB of RAM with 80 GB of disk, which is where the per‑GB curve flattens out.

## The $1.60 plan is the most expensive way to get IPv4

This is the number that decides whether the cheap tier makes sense for you.

HDD VPS IPv6 costs **$1.60 per month** on annual billing. It ships without an IPv4 address. An IPv4 address is a separate line item at **$3.31 per month**. Add them and you're at **$4.91 per month** — $58.92 for the year.

HDD VPS Start, which is a near‑identical machine (1 core, 512 MB, 5 GB HDD) and comes with IPv4 and IPv6, costs **$2.60 per month** annual, or $31.20 for the year.

The cheap tier costs $27.72 more per year the moment you need IPv4. The same math applies one tier up: NVMe VPS IPv6 at $2.38 plus $3.31 is $5.69 per month, against $3.38 for NVMe VPS Start++ with IPv4 included.

So the IPv6‑only group has one legitimate use case: a box that only ever talks to other IPv6 hosts, or a test machine you reach through a jump host you already own. For anything serving normal visitors, buy from the IPv4 + IPv6 group and skip the add‑on entirely.

## 300 Mbit fair‑share: what the port number means

Every plan card lists an "unmetered port" with a fair‑share note — 300, 500 or 750 Mbit on the Special offers, and "93+ TB monthly" style equivalents next to it. Traffic is unlimited on all plans; you pay for disk space, not for gigabytes moved.

The port speed is a shared ceiling, and the page says so with the fair‑share label. It is not a committed rate. If you need committed bandwidth, JustHost sells it separately:

| Add‑on | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Sysadmin services | $9.16 / hour |
| IPv4 address | $3.31 / month |
| +10 Mbps bandwidth | $2.50 / month |
| Guaranteed 1 Gbps bandwidth | $333.33 / month |

A guaranteed 1 Gbps costs more per month than the most expensive plan in the list. Unless you're moving serious sustained throughput for a business that depends on it, the fair‑share port is what you'll be using, and on a 300 Mbit shared port a small site or API will rarely notice.

## HDD vs SSD vs NVMe: the real price of the storage upgrade

The storage choice is priced in small increments, which makes it easy to overpay or underpay without noticing.

At the entry tier (1 core / 512 MB / 5 GB), moving from HDD to SSD costs $0.52 per month on annual billing ($2.60 → $3.12), and SSD to NVMe another $0.26 ($3.12 → $3.38). At the Standard tier (2 cores / 2 GB / 25 GB), HDD to SSD is $1.76 ($8.80 → $10.56) and SSD to NVMe $0.88 ($10.56 → $11.44).

Half a dollar a month for NVMe on a $2.60 server is worth taking. On the larger tiers the SSD premium grows faster than the NVMe premium, so if you're already paying for Standard or Super, going straight to NVMe costs less than the HDD‑to‑SSD jump would suggest.

One thing the marketing copy and the price page disagree on: the FAQ states that JustHost VPS "runs on fast SSD and NVMe drives", while the price page sells HDD plans alongside them. Read the plan name, not the FAQ sentence.

## No automatic backups, and what to do about it

JustHost's own FAQ answers this one plainly: automatic backups are not offered, and the recommendation is to set up your own backup server and keep copies on a separate machine.

That's a real operational cost, and it's the item most price comparisons leave out. A $2.25 VPS with no snapshot feature means you need a second destination for backups — another cheap VPS, object storage elsewhere, or a machine at home — plus a cron job and a restore test you've actually run. Budget an hour of setup and a few dollars a month, and treat the restore test as mandatory, because an untested backup is a guess.

The rest of the included feature list is solid for the price: free IPv6 address, unlimited traffic, SSH access with git and bash, built‑in DDoS filtering that the FAQ describes as traffic monitoring plus mechanisms that stop attacks as they begin, and 24/7 support.

## Which JustHost VPS plan fits your workload

**A blog, a small site, a cron‑driven scraper, a Telegram bot.** Sirius Special at $2.25. 1 GB of RAM and 20 GB of NVMe covers a static site generator, a WordPress install with a cache plugin, or a Python script that runs on a schedule.

**The same, plus MySQL and a bit of headroom.** Arcturus Special at $3.21. Doubling RAM for under a dollar is the cheapest upgrade decision in the whole table.

**Several Docker containers, a staging environment, a mid‑size database.** Vega Special at $6.23. 4 GB of RAM and 80 GB of NVMe is where you stop worrying about memory during a build.

**A box that only needs to exist, and you already have IPv6.** HDD VPS IPv6 at $1.60. Nothing cheaper in the lineup, and nothing more likely to frustrate you if you misjudge the IPv4 requirement.

**You need IPv4, the workload is tiny, and every dollar counts.** HDD VPS Start at $2.60. It beats the IPv6 tier plus an IPv4 add‑on by $2.31 per month, and it's the honest answer for anyone serving a normal audience on a shoestring.

**Anything above 8 GB of RAM.** Yildun Special ($12.96), Betelgeuse Special ($21.12) and Altair Special ($37.92) are priced close to linearly per GB, so pick by measured RAM use rather than by future hopes. Upgrading later is possible without moving data, so starting small is not a trap.

A note on the middle of the table: the IPv4 + IPv6 group gives you 4 GB of RAM for $22.88 per month (NVMe VPS Super++), while Vega Special gives 4 GB for $6.23 with more disk and a stated 500 Mbit port. The core counts differ (4 vs 2) and the IPv4 + IPv6 cards don't publish a port speed, so the two aren't interchangeable — but if RAM is your bottleneck and cores aren't, the Special offers line is dramatically cheaper.

## JustHost VPS FAQ

**Can I change the server location after ordering?**
Yes. Location switching is done from the client area at any time, per the features listed on every plan.

**Can I change the IP address?**
Yes, also from the client area, at any time.

**Does migration from another host cost anything?**
No. JustHost moves files, databases and server settings for free, with the stated goal of no downtime or data loss. You give them access to your current hosting and they handle the transfer.

**Can I add CPU, RAM or disk later?**
Yes. Configuration changes are applied without moving your data to another server, with minimal downtime. That's the reason starting on a small plan is low‑risk.

**How do I access the server?**
SSH. The feature list calls out copying and editing files, using git, and running bash scripts.

**Is DDoS protection included?**
Yes, on every plan, described as a multi‑layered approach combining traffic monitoring with mechanisms that stop attacks as they begin.

**Is there a control panel?**
The FAQ refers to managing the server "from an easy-to-use control panel" without naming it, so confirm the panel and OS template list during checkout if that matters to your setup.

**Can I cancel easily?**
The feature list includes easy cancellation. Renewal pricing, refund terms and payment methods aren't documented on the price page, so read them on the order form before you pay.

## Before you order

Match the plan to measured usage, not to ambition: 1 GB of RAM handles a small site, 2 GB handles a small site with a database, and 4 GB handles containers and builds. Everything above that is a bet on traffic you don't have yet, and upgrades don't require a migration.
